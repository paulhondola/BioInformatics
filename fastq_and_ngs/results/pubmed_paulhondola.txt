Title: Exploration of Key Targets and Prognostic Significance of Vitamin Metabolism in Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma Progression Using Bulk and Single-Cell RNA Sequencing.
Authors: Jiang S, Xia Q
Abstract: BACKGROUND: The incidence of Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma (UCEC) is increasing, and the 5-year survival rate for patients with advanced-stage or high-grade disease remains low, underscoring the urgent need for reliable molecular prognostic biomarkers. Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer, yet the prognostic role of Vitamin Metabolism-Related Genes (VMRGs) in UCEC remains unclear. METHODS: Transcriptomic and clinical data for UCEC were retrieved from the TCGA database, while scRNA-seq datasets were procured from the GEO database. WGCNA was used to identify VMRG activity-related co-expression modules. The scRNA-seq dataset was utilized for cell type annotation, malignant cell identification via inferCNV, and cell-cell communication analysis via CellChat. Genes from WGCNA modules and DEGs from malignant cells were intersected to establish a prognostic model with LASSO-Cox regression. Differences in the immune microenvironment, genomic mutations, and drug sensitivity between high- and low-risk groups were further analyzed. qRT-PCR was used to detect the expression of the model genes; a DKC1-knockdown UCEC cell model was constructed, and the effects of DKC1 on cell functions were verified by qRT-PCR, Western blot, CCK-8, colony formation, Transwell, and ELISA assays. RESULTS: WGCNA linked MEgreen and MElightcyan modules to VMRG activity. Malignant epithelial cells showed higher VMRG activity, with IL6 signaling as a key TME-modulating axis. An 8-gene LASSO-Cox model (MT-CO1, MTHFD2, MRPS22, DKC1, TFG, TINF2, CHCHD7, HEBP1) stratified high-risk patients with increased MDSC infiltration and TP53 mutations. High-risk group was sensitive to BI-2536, ML210, obatoclax; low-risk to cediranib, tanespimycin, VAF-347. Cell-based experiments showed that the model genes were differentially expressed; moreover, DKC1 enhanced the sensitivity to BI-2536 and promoted UCEC malignant progression by activating the IL-6/JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway. CONCLUSION: The 8-gene prognostic signature developed in this study, associated with VMRG activity, effectively stratifies the risk for UCEC patients, providing a novel reference for prognostic assessment and individualized treatment.

Title: Genetic Correlates of Malignant Behavior in Aggressive Pituitary Tumors.
Authors: Theodorou A, Benhamida J, Alano T, Armstrong D, Drodge S, Bellantoni M, Braun K, Chen J, Chow F, Drincic A, Goichot B, Heaney AP, Magge R, Majd NK, Nandakumar S, Nelson C, Reiner A, Reisch A, Silverstein JM, Uzilov A, Wardlaw SL, Rosenblum M, Richards A, Tabar V, Geer EB, Lin AL
Abstract: OBJECTIVE: Aggressive pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs) that progress despite surgery, radiotherapy (RT), and medical therapy are heterogenous; only a subset exhibits overtly malignant behavior. We examined genetic determinants of metastatic behavior and poor prognosis. DESIGN: Retrospective genomic cohort study of 39 patients (62 tumor samples) with aggressive PitNETs. METHODS: Next-generation sequencing was performed using the MSK-IMPACT platform on a sequencing protocol. Genetic alterations were correlated with metastatic status, and progression-free and overall survival (PFS and OS) from first progression following RT. RESULTS: Cell-cycle alterations were found in 23.1% of patients and were more frequently identified in metastatic compared to non-metastatic tumors (42.1% vs 5.0%; OR=12.9; p=0.008). TP53, ATRX, DAXX, and/or SF3B1 alterations were associated with adverse clinical outcomes but did not differ by metastatic status (53% of metastatic vs 60% of non-metastatic tumors). Within the corticotroph lineage, gene-level alterations in TP53, ATRX, and/or DAXX captured corticotrophs with recurrent chromosomal loss-of-heterozygosity (rcLOH), and were associated with shorter PFS (10.1 vs 149.9 months; p=0.009) and OS (116.6 months vs not reached [NR]; p=0.022). A subset of corticotrophs lacking rcLOH harbored gain-of-function USP8 mutations and were associated with longer PFS (149.9 vs 11.5 months; p=0.022). In lactotroph PitNETs, TP53, ATRX, and/or SF3B1 mutations were associated with shorter PFS (4.6 vs 21.5 months; p=0.049). CONCLUSIONS: Cell-cycle alterations were enriched in metastatic PitNETs and may contribute to metastatic dissemination. Next-generation sequencing segregates aggressive PitNETs into prognostic subgroups.

Title: Real-World Experience of Using Next-Generation-Sequencing in Multiple Myeloma Diagnostics in the UK National Health Service.
Authors: Beer SA, Seneca E, Bertuglia G, Proszek P, Thornton T, Hubank M, Patrizan MV, El-Sharkawi D, Smith K, Pawlyn C, Boyd K, Kaiser MF
Abstract: The IMS-IMWG 2025 updated definition of high-risk multiple myeloma (HRMM) introduced next-generation sequencing (NGS) based detection of TP53 single-nucleotide variants (SNV) as an independent high-risk factor. However, most published data on TP53 SNVs in MM derive from exploratory research studies using NGS interpretation pipelines outside of quality-controlled, clinically accredited laboratories, with limited information on co-occurring high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (HRCA). To address this, we retrospectively reviewed all transplant-eligible newly diagnosed MM (NDMM) patients treated at the Royal Marsden Hospital in the UK between February 2021 and April 2026 who underwent standard-of-care parallel fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing for all IMS-IMWG consensus genomic staging (CGS) criteria and NGS-based TP53 SNV calling in the National Health Service diagnostically accredited Genomic Laboratory Hub (GLH). A total of 102 patients were included. Based on FISH, 24 patients (23.5%) fulfilled IMS-IMWG CGS criteria for HRMM, including 8 (7.8%) with del(17p) and 5 (4.9%) with biallelic del(1p32). All 24 patients had co-occurrence of 2+ HRCA, including all 5 patients with biallelic del(1p32). NGS identified 4 cases (3.9%) with TP53 mutations, of which 2 harbored del(17p), while 2 were classified as HRMM solely based on NGS results. However, both isolated TP53mut also carried additional HRCA. Thus, in this standard-of-care diagnostic setting, only 2% of NDMM were re-classified based on NGS findings and, notably, all 26 HRMM had 2+ high-risk lesions. Although limited by cohort size and lack of survival outcomes, these descriptive data demonstrate genomic co-occurrence rather than independent prognostic value and support a continued focus on reliable, quality-controlled detection of structural variants alongside NGS.

Title: Transformation to Small-Cell Lung Carcinoma and Then to Sarcomatoid Carcinoma in an EGFR-Mutant Lung Adenocarcinoma Patient During Systemic Therapy: A Case Report.
Authors: Huang B, Lu T, Hong R, Liu X, Gao X, Xu Y, Chen M, Zhong W, Wang M, Zhao J
Abstract: This case exemplifies stepwise histological transformations of an initially EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma driven by tumor lineage plasticity under therapeutic pressure. Initial resistance to osimertinib manifested as transformation to combined small-cell lung carcinoma, associated with concurrent TP53/RB1 co-inactivation and the acquisition of an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) C797S mutation. Subsequent progression after chemotherapy featured a rare sarcomatoid transformation, with the tumor retaining the original EGFR Exon 19 deletion, supporting clonal evolution. This phenotypic shift, indicative of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), may have been associated with PTEN loss and potential activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. The serial emergence of neuroendocrine and sarcomatoid phenotypes underscores the critical need for rebiopsy to guide therapy and highlights the profound adaptability of EGFR-mutant tumors, which remain a major therapeutic challenge.

Title: Cytotoxic and Pro-Apoptotic Effects of the Compound 2-Carboxy-2-Heptano-Indolin-3-One in HeLa Cell Spheroids.
Authors: Rocha ALC, Westin CF, Niekawa ETG, Andrade G, de Lima LVA, Risson LK, da Silva MF, Alarcon SF, Mantovani MS
Abstract: Indolinones are compounds with recognized antineoplastic properties and can act as inhibitors of multiple kinases. However, the antitumor effects of 2-carboxy-2-heptano-indolin-3-one (IDC) have not yet been investigated in cervical cancer models. This study investigated the cytotoxic and pro-apoptotic effects of IDC in HeLa cells, with emphasis on a three-dimensional spheroid model, and evaluated its effects on spheroid growth, cell cycle distribution, apoptosis, and the expression of genes associated with cell death and DNA damage responses. Cell viability was assessed in monolayer cultures using the resazurin assay, while spheroid growth and morphology were evaluated by microscopy and volumetric analysis. Apoptosis and cell cycle distribution were investigated by flow cytometry, and gene expression was assessed by RT-qPCR. IDC reduced cell viability by up to 80% in monolayer cultures and significantly inhibited spheroid growth, with loss of structural integrity and absence of recovery after treatment withdrawal. Flow cytometry revealed 81.7-83.6% apoptotic cells following IDC treatment, compared with 35.8% in the vehicle control. No significant changes in G1, S, or G2/M cell cycle distribution were observed. RT-qPCR showed increased mRNA levels of CASP8, CASP9, CASP3, BBC3, and GADD45A and decreased levels of TP53, CDKN1A, and MYC. These transcriptional changes suggest the involvement of apoptotic signaling and DNA damage response mechanisms. Overall, IDC exhibited cytotoxic and pro-apoptotic effects in HeLa cells and impaired three-dimensional spheroid growth and integrity, supporting further investigation of its antineoplastic potential in cervical cancer.
